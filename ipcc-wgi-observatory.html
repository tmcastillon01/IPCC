What does IPCC Working Group I actually do?

It reads the world's climate science, checks it, and writes down what we know for sure. Governments then use that as their shared facts.

How a report gets made

The IPCC does not run its own experiments. It is more like the world's most careful editor.

  1. Scientists publishThousands of studies on oceans, ice, air and carbon.
  2. Authors weigh it upExperts from around the world read it all and judge how sure we can be.
  3. Reviewers check itOther experts and governments comment on every draft.
  4. Governments approveThey agree the summary line by line, so everyone trusts it.

Working Group I covers the physical science: what is changing, how fast, and why. Group II covers impacts and Group III covers solutions.

Three questions it answers

Question 1

Where did the extra heat go?

Gases from our fuel and farming act like a blanket. The extra heat has to go somewhere. Mostly, it goes into the ocean.

1 square = 1% of the extra heat, 1971–2018. Source: AR6 WGI Ch. 7, Table 7.1

Question 2

Where does our CO₂ go?

Picture a bathtub with the tap on full and a drain that is too small. Land and ocean drain just over half. The rest stays in the air and the water level keeps rising.

Tap: CO₂ we emit each year
46% stays in the air
31%soaked up by land
23%soaked up by ocean

Why it matters: what stays in the air keeps trapping heat. That is why the water level, not just the tap, is what scientists watch.

Process note

How I built this

  • Every figure traces to AR6 Working Group I. The sources table names the chapter or Summary for Policymakers paragraph, so a reviewer can check a number in a minute.
  • Numbers keep their ranges. +2.7°C for SSP2-4.5 always travels with its very likely range of +2.1°C to +3.5°C.
  • I use the IPCC’s calibrated words (high confidence, very likely) only where the report uses them, and I don’t strengthen them for effect.
  • The AR7 details were checked against ipcc.ch pages in November 2026. The 2028 delivery date is shown as the Co-Chairs’ proposal, because the Panel had not agreed it.
  • Nobody at the IPCC has reviewed this. In real work each figure would go to the relevant chapter team or the TSU before it went public.
