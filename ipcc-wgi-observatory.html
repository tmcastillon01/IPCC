The Physical Science
of Climate Change

Three questions the IPCC’s Working Group I answers: where the heat goes, where our CO₂ goes, and how warm it gets.

First time hearing about Working Group I? It is the part of the IPCC that explains the physical science. Here are the three answers. Each is drawn as a circle of small shapes you can count.

1 · Where does the extra heat go?Almost all of it, 91%, goes into the ocean.

2 · Where does our CO₂ go?Nearly half stays in the air. The rest goes into land and sea.

3 · How warm could it get?It depends on the emissions path we choose.

In one line: the ocean takes 91% of the extra heat, 46% of our CO₂ stays in the air, and by 2081–2100 the world is projected to be +1.4°C to +4.4°C warmer, depending on emissions.

What is an SSP? A Shared Socioeconomic Pathway is a story of how the world might develop: energy use, population and policy. The second number (1.9, 4.5, 8.5) is the heating effect in 2100, in watts per square metre. Low means a small push on the climate, high means a big one. Tap a pathway to isolate it.

What this does not show. Every number is a best estimate with a range: SSP2-4.5 is +2.7°C, very likely between +2.1°C and +3.5°C. These are global averages, so your region can differ a lot (the Atlas shows regions). The observed +1.09°C is measured for 2011–2020. The warming pathways are projections, not forecasts of which one we will follow.

How a report gets made

The IPCC does not run its own experiments. It is more like the world’s most careful editor.

1

Scientists publish

Thousands of studies on oceans, ice, air and carbon.

2

Authors weigh it up

Experts from around the world read it all and judge how sure we can be.

3

Reviewers check it

Other experts and governments comment on every draft.

4

Governments approve

They agree the summary line by line, so everyone trusts it.

234authorsScientists who wrote the AR6 physical science report.
66countriesSo no single country’s view dominates.
14,000+studies assessedRead and weighed before a sentence is final.

Working Group I covers the physical science: what is changing, how fast, and why. Group II covers impacts and Group III covers solutions.

Process note

How I built this

  • Every figure traces to AR6 Working Group I. The sources table names the chapter or Summary for Policymakers paragraph, so a reviewer can check a number in a minute.
  • Numbers keep their ranges. +2.7°C for SSP2-4.5 always travels with its very likely range of +2.1°C to +3.5°C.
  • I use the IPCC’s calibrated words (high confidence, very likely) only where the report uses them, and I don’t strengthen them for effect.
  • The AR7 details were checked against ipcc.ch pages in November 2026. The 2028 delivery date is shown as the Co-Chairs’ proposal, because the Panel had not agreed it.
  • Nobody at the IPCC has reviewed this. In real work each figure would go to the relevant chapter team or the TSU before it went public.
