IPCC Working Group I:
The Physical Science Basis

Synthesizing Earth System Energy, Carbon & Warming Trajectories (AR6 Outputs & AR7 Cycle)

In plain terms: three questions the IPCC answers about the physical climate. Where does the extra heat go? Where does our CO₂ go? How warm could it get? Each answer is drawn as a circle of small shapes you can count.

Each planetary radiative lens encodes a foundational quantitative pillar of the IPCC Working Group I physical science assessment. Every individual spectral prism represents 1% of Earth’s excess energy or anthropogenic CO₂ flux, or 0.1°C of projected surface warming, while luminous radiative flux beams trace the physical coupling across reservoirs.

AR7 context: The WGI Technical Support Unit is hosted by Université Paris-Saclay. Lead Author Meetings so far: Paris (Dec 2025, joint with WGII and WGIII), Santiago (Apr 2026) and Arusha (Nov 2026). The Co-Chairs propose delivering the report in 2028; the Panel has not yet agreed the date. The figures mapped below are AR6 outputs.

How to read it

four steps, about thirty seconds
  1. Find the three lenses. Each large circle answers one question: where Earth’s extra heat goes (left), where our CO₂ goes (right), and how warm the planet gets (bottom).
  2. Count the prisms. Every small coloured shape is one unit. On the heat and carbon lenses one prism is 1% of the total, so 91 blue prisms means 91%. On the temperature lens one prism is 0.1°C.
  3. Match colour to place. Blue is ocean, green is land, pale blue is ice, orange is air. On the temperature lens, colour marks the emissions pathway, from teal (very low) to crimson (very high).
  4. Hover or tap for the evidence. Every prism, lens and probe opens a note with its number and where it comes from. Press Synthesize Earth System to merge the three lenses, and use the scenario buttons to isolate one warming pathway.
Key takeaway

The ocean holds 91% of the extra heat, nearly half of our CO₂ stays in the air, and the emissions pathway we follow decides whether 2081–2100 sits near +1.4°C or +4.4°C.

Read with care

  • The observed ring (+1.09°C) is measured data for 2011–2020. The other rings are projections for 2081–2100.
  • Every number is a best estimate with a range. SSP2-4.5, for example, is +2.7°C, very likely between +2.1°C and +3.5°C.
  • The centre circles show totals, not prisms. The heat total is about 435 ZJ, so 1% is roughly 4.35 ZJ.
  • The orbiting probes are context about the AR7 process and tools. They are not measurements.

Why it matters, and where it comes from

every number can be traced

Why these three findings

Working Group I assesses the physical science of climate change: what is happening, how fast, and why. These three findings answer the questions people ask first. Where is the heat? Where does our carbon end up? How much warming do different emissions choices lead to?

AR6 is the current published assessment. AR7 is being written now and will update these answers, which is why the observatory also shows the AR7 milestones.

What this page is, and is not

It is an independent communication sample: an attempt to make three headline findings readable at a glance while keeping every number traceable.

It is not an official IPCC product and has not been reviewed by the IPCC. Figures are rounded to whole prisms. Follow the links below for full ranges and confidence levels.
