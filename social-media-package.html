Climate science for people who never opened the report

A strategy and three working post mock-ups (LinkedIn carousel, Facebook, X) built from IPCC AR6 WGI baselines. Swipe, tap and expand them the way a reader would. Independent sample; not official IPCC material.

Strategy

Strategy for non-science audiences

Everyday professionals, parents, local community members, students and busy readers care about food costs, heat, water and local planning. They tune out acronyms. The plan starts from that.

Lead with analogies, then anchor the number

Open on a mental model (a sink with a narrow drain for CO₂, the ocean as a heat buffer). Put the figure second, with its source on the card.

Separate “what we know” from “what we do”

Keep physical baselines plain and sourced. Then invite reflection on choices and preparedness, without lecturing and without endorsing any policy.

Hook in the first two lines

Feeds fold long captions behind “…more”. Every caption opens with the finding and saves the explanation for the expand.

End on one question

Each post closes with a single, jargon-free question. Comments show whether the translation worked.

Platform mix

The guidance note names only Twitter and Facebook. The package now plans for LinkedIn, Facebook and X/Instagram with a format per platform. Short video is the next format to test.

Guidance note · p.2
Update

Evidence base is 2018 and UK/US-led

The handbook says so itself. Treat its frames as hypotheses and test them with the audience you are actually writing for before scaling.

Handbook · p.4, p.12
Update

Scepticism is no longer the only risk

The handbook says not to worry unduly about scepticism. Comment sections now also carry misinformation, so each post needs a reply plan and a sources line.

Handbook · p.7 (a judgement call, not a quoted finding)
Update

Report-figure rules, adapted for feeds

The style guide targets printed figures: Arial, no bold, 9 or 11 pt, 9 or 18 cm wide, CMYK. Social cards need RGB, 4:5 format and larger type. This sample uses its own typefaces; an official WGI product would follow the guide's fonts.

Style guide · Steps 4, 6, 12
Update

Check the current guidance note

The note was written for the AR6 cycle and looks ahead to the 2021 SPM approval. Confirm what applies in AR7 before publishing anything real.

Guidance note · p.2
Add

Say in what capacity you speak

Label personal views as personal and keep policy opinions out. Every post here is marked as an independent sample, not an IPCC product.

Guidance note · p.1; Handbook · Principle 1
Add

Cite published findings only

Nothing from meetings or drafts. The cover previously cited a Lead Author Meeting as a source; it now cites AR6 chapters only.

Guidance note · p.1–2
Add

A human story and a “therefore”

The Facebook caption now follows the handbook's And/But/Therefore template. Narrative should end on something people can do, or it feels overwhelming.

Handbook · Principle 4
Add

Real people, local images

The cliff cover is the classic landscape look. For live posts, pair findings with authentic local photos of people, per the Climate Visuals principles.

Handbook · Principle 6
Add

Test before you post

Ask three people from the target audience what the main message is. If their answer differs from the intent, redraft. Use a fresh person each round.

Style guide · Step 10 (M.A.D.E.)
Add

Standalone captions, controllable motion

The first sentence works as the title, and the caption stays well under the guide's 250 words. Motion is triggered by the reader, and reduced-motion settings are respected.

Why this post is built this way

  • The cover states the finding and a question, so the swipe has a reason.
  • One idea per card; every card shows its source line.
  • Interactive elements let readers test the number (tap a bar, pick a scenario) instead of just reading it.
  • The caption's first two lines work even if nobody taps “…more”.
  • The reel puts pause, replay and skip in the reader's hands, as the style guide advises for animation.

Branding

One look across every post

The brand is deliberately independent. It borrows the IPCC's accessibility rules for data colour, but never its logo, wordmark or typefaces, so no reader mistakes it for an official product.

ActionWhat you can do

Do

Keep a gold top bar, a sources line on every card and the “independent sample” label on the last card. Use one message per card.

Don't

Don't use the IPCC logo or wordmark, red and green together, rainbow scales, or icons that rely on one culture, such as wheat and tractors for farming.

If this became official

Switch to the TSU's visual identity, Arial or Frutiger for figure text, and the IPCC colour files, and route posts through the WGI communications team.
